# Deal close

Use when the deal has all four factors (Champion, Buyer, Pain, Trigger) and is entering the decision stage.

Sources: TheCraigHewitt sales-skills, LeadMagic founder-sales (MIT). Do not copy code.

Read the most recent `../shared/03_Brand/<project-name>/deals/<name>-*.md` and `sales-context.md`.

## Principles

> Do not create friction at the moment they want to buy. Make "yes" as easy as possible.

> Do not discount out of fear. If price is the problem, it is a value problem, not a number problem.

## Pre-close checklist

- [ ] Have you spoken to the budget holder (buyer), not just the champion?
- [ ] Do they already know the price from a previous session (no sticker shock)?
- [ ] Is the next step after agreement clear (where to sign, when to start)?
- [ ] Is there anything still blocking them that you do not know about?

## Simple proposal

For small products (1–2 users, price < 5M VND / $200 USD/month): no long proposal needed. A short email or message is enough:

```
Hi [name],

Summary of what we agreed:
- Product: [name]
- Plan: [plan and price]
- Start date: [date]
- Next step: [payment link / how to start]

Happy to answer any questions.
```

Draft this email from deal-brief and sales-context.

## Handling a discount request

If they ask for a discount:
- Ask: "What level would let you decide today?"
- If discounting: exchange equivalent value (shorter term, fewer features) — do not simply cut the price.
- Maximum threshold: decide this before the meeting. Below it, decline politely.

## After the close meeting

- Agreement: write the date, plan, and price in `deals/<name>-<date>.md`. Set status to "CLOSED".
- Rejection: run `win-loss` to learn.
- Further delay: is there a concrete next step? No next step likely means a dead deal.
