# Discovery prep

Method sources: SPICED (Winning by Design), SPIN Selling (Neil Rackham), AIDevGTM founder-led-sales (MIT). Do not copy code.

Read `../shared/03_Brand/<project-name>/sales-context.md` first.

## Discovery principles

> Talk 30%, listen 70%. The best questioner controls the conversation.

The goal of the first meeting is **not** to persuade. The goal is to confirm whether all four factors are present:
1. **Champion:** Who will use it and wants to buy?
2. **Buyer:** Who controls the budget?
3. **Pain with cost:** What is it costing them (money, time, risk)?
4. **Trigger:** Why must it be solved now and not in three months?

If any one of the four is still blank, this is not yet a deal. It is a conversation that needs more information.

## Input

- Name and role of the person to meet (if known).
- Product name and domain.

## Steps

**1. Pre-meeting research (if information is available)**
- What are they doing that relates to the problem the product solves?
- Any signal they have this pain? (from prior conversation, profile, actions they have taken)

**2. SPICED question set** (written for this specific product)

| Group | Suggested question |
|---|---|
| Situation | "How are you currently handling [task X]?" |
| Problem | "Which part of that process takes the most time?" |
| Impact | "If you had to estimate, how much is that costing you in [time/money/opportunity]?" |
| Critical event | "Is there a deadline or event that makes this urgent to solve soon?" |
| Decision | "If we find a good fit today, what would the next step look like?" |

**3. Opening line** (not a pitch)
One sentence about something specific you have observed about them, then ask one question.

**4. What to confirm by end of meeting**
- Four factors: Champion, Buyer, Pain, Trigger — each one clear or not.
- A concrete next step with a date.
