# Value proposition

Sources: Ananas B2B-GTM-Suite (MIT), AIDevGTM gtm-cofounder (MIT). Do not copy code.

Read `../shared/03_Brand/<project-name>/sales-context.md` first. If missing, run `sales-context` first.

## What a strong VP looks like

A strong VP: the listener immediately recognizes **their own problem**, sees **how the product solves it**, and can picture **a specific result**. No words like "powerful", "seamless", "easy to use", or "best in class" without accompanying evidence.

**Signs of a weak VP:**
- Describes a feature instead of an outcome ("has feature X")
- Generic outcome ("saves time")
- Listener has to figure out what the product actually does

## Steps

1. Read the current VPs in `sales-context.md`.
2. For each VP, check three questions:
   - **P (Problem):** Is the problem specific? Will the customer recognize it as their problem?
   - **S (Solution):** Is the solution clear? Does the customer understand what the product does?
   - **O (Outcome):** Is the result measurable or clearly felt? Is there evidence?
3. Rewrite weak VPs. Keep strong VPs unchanged.
4. Rank: which VP leads first with the chosen ICP?

## Final check

Try reading the VP to someone who knows nothing about the product. If they ask "what does it do?" after hearing it, the VP is not clear enough.
