# Objection handling

Sources: TheCraigHewitt sales-skills (MIT), Chris Voss Tactical Empathy, AIDevGTM founder-led-sales (MIT). Do not copy code.

Read `../shared/03_Brand/<project-name>/sales-context.md` first.

## Principles

> "Let me think about it" is not an objection. It is a missing next step, or a concern that has not been voiced yet.

A real objection = an unanswered concern. Not a rejection. Someone who objects is still considering.

Forbidden: do not argue, do not discount immediately out of fear.

## Four common objection types

### 1. Price ("Too expensive", "No budget yet")
Not always a money issue. Often a value-clarity issue.

**Ask instead of defending:**
- "Expensive compared to what?" → Understand what they are comparing to.
- "If it solves [problem X], is [result Y] worth it?" → Bring it back to outcome.
- "Where does budget for [problem X] typically live in your company?" → Find the real buyer.

Never discount the first time just because they say expensive. Discounting before they buy teaches them the price is not real.

### 2. Timing ("Not the right time now", "Wait until next quarter")
**Ask:**
- "What needs to happen for next quarter to be the right time?" → Identify the real trigger.
- "In the next 90 days, if [problem] goes unsolved, what happens?" → Clarify the cost of waiting.

### 3. Competition ("I'm already using [competitor]", "We have [A]")
Do not attack the competitor. Ask about the gap:
- "What do you like most about [competitor]?" → Listen first.
- "Is there anything [competitor] hasn't fully solved for you?" → Find the gap.
- "[Competitor] is good at X. Customers choose [our product] when they need Y, because [specific evidence]."

### 4. "Let me think about it" / silence
Signal of insufficient confidence or unclear next step.
- "What do you need to be certain about before deciding?"
- "Does anyone else need to be involved in the decision?" → Find the hidden buyer.
- "Can I send you something to help you explain it internally?" → Arm the champion.
