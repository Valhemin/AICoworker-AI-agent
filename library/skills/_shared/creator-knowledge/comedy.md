# Comedy Design Playbook

## Purpose

Build comedy from expectation, character, timing, status, specificity, and consequence rather than random jokes or exaggerated wording.

## Core mechanisms

- **incongruity**: the result violates a prepared expectation;
- **reversal**: power, knowledge, or intent changes direction;
- **status**: a character protects, gains, loses, or misreads status;
- **escalation**: the same rule produces increasingly costly outcomes;
- **misdirection**: attention is guided toward a plausible but incomplete interpretation;
- **recognition**: a precise observation exposes a shared behavior;
- **commitment**: a character continues a bad tactic because identity or status is at stake;
- **callback**: an earlier detail returns with changed meaning;
- **contrast**: performance, image, sound, or edit contradicts the stated situation.

## Joke and beat anatomy

```yaml
comic_beat:
  normal_expectation:
  planted_information:
  character_intent:
  pressure:
  turn_or_reveal:
  consequence:
  tag_or_callback:
  target_of_joke:
```

The target should be clear. “Punching down” is not a technical rule that resolves every context, but comedy aimed at a less powerful identity group carries greater cultural and brand risk than comedy aimed at behavior, hypocrisy, systems, or self-deception.

## Character comedy

Give each recurring character:

- a status strategy;
- a blind spot;
- a preferred tactic;
- a line they will not admit crossing;
- a speech rhythm;
- a relationship that creates pressure.

A joke becomes stronger when it is the inevitable behavior of this character, not an interchangeable one-liner.

## Timing

- setup must provide exactly enough information;
- delay can create tension, but extra explanation kills inference;
- reaction shots are often the payoff;
- silence, breath, framing, and sound can carry the joke;
- edit on realization, failed concealment, or status change rather than automatically on the spoken punchline;
- leave room for audience comprehension before the next information-heavy beat.

## Short-form comedy

For 10–45 seconds, use one clear premise and escalate in two or three turns. A hook may be a recognizable situation, an impossible rule, a status threat, or an immediately visible contradiction. End at the strongest consequence; do not append a generic CTA that breaks the comic world.

## Global adaptation

Humor is highly context-dependent. Validate:

- taboo topics and power relationships;
- irony tolerance and literal comprehension;
- references, slang, accents, and wordplay;
- pacing and performance conventions;
- whether the joke survives subtitles or dubbing;
- whether localization requires a new mechanism rather than translated wording.

Prefer transcreation: preserve the comic function, not the lexical structure.

## Writer’s room protocol

1. Generate premise variants, not merely punchline variants.
2. Label the mechanism and target of each joke.
3. Read aloud or perform a scratch version.
4. Remove explanatory lines.
5. Test with a small target-audience sample where stakes justify it.
6. Track what failed: confusion, predictability, offense, timing, performance, or weak consequence.

## Failure recovery

- predictable: strengthen misdirection or character commitment;
- random: reconnect the turn to planted information;
- mean: redirect target toward behavior/status/system and restore consequence symmetry;
- too verbal: move setup or payoff into image, performance, prop, sound, or edit;
- untranslatable: replace the local reference while preserving recognition or reversal;
- weak ending: escalate the consequence or use a callback instead of explaining.

## Acceptance criteria

The audience can understand the premise, the turn is prepared but not obvious, the joke belongs to the character or situation, performance timing is executable, and market/brand risks are explicitly reviewed.
