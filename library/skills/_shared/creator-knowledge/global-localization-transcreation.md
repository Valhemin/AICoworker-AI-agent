# Global Localization and Transcreation Playbook

## Goal

Create content that performs the same strategic and emotional function in a target market, even when the words, examples, visuals, pacing, offer, or structure must change.

## Four modes

1. **Translation** — meaning is stable and cultural distance is low.
2. **Adaptation** — language plus units, examples, layouts, and minor cultural details change.
3. **Transcreation** — hook, joke, metaphor, CTA, proof order, and sometimes scenes are rebuilt to preserve function.
4. **Original-local creation** — the source concept is unsuitable; create from target-market evidence.

Choose the mode before writing. Do not label transcreation as translation.

## Functional decomposition

For each important element, document what it does:

| Element | Source form | Function | Constraint | Target-market option |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hook | phrase/visual | stop, orient, create tension | must not mislead | local equivalent mechanism |
| joke | wordplay | release tension, signal identity | may not translate | new joke with same social function |
| proof | testimonial/data/demo | reduce doubt | legal and availability constraints | local proof or disclosure |
| CTA | imperative | prompt next action | platform and offer | locally natural action language |

## Language-naturalness checks

- Does it sound written or spoken for the intended format?
- Is register consistent with age, profession, relationship, and brand?
- Is code-switching intentional and authentic?
- Are pronouns, honorifics, politeness, and directness appropriate?
- Do subtitles fit reading speed and screen space?
- Does text expansion break design or timing?
- Are idioms, puns, rhyme, and alliteration rebuilt rather than copied?

Back-translation checks semantic loss but does not prove naturalness or persuasion.

## Cultural review dimensions

- power distance and authority cues;
- individual versus family/community framing;
- humor targets and face-saving;
- gender, religion, politics, class, ethnicity, disability, and age representation;
- food, clothing, gestures, colors, symbols, seasons, holidays, and geography;
- local trust signals and objection patterns;
- taboo or regulated claims;
- visual familiarity without cliché.

These dimensions are questions, not fixed country stereotypes. Validate with evidence and local reviewers.

## Multi-market asset design

Build the master for localization:

- separate voice, music, and SFX stems;
- keep text editable and avoid baked-in text where possible;
- provide text-safe zones for expansion and RTL layouts;
- create clean plates and alternate product shots;
- isolate cultural references that may need replacement;
- record phonetic guides, names, and pronunciation;
- maintain a termbase and do-not-translate list;
- version each locale separately.

## Evaluation

Use two distinct reviews:

1. **Meaning/claim review** — accuracy, omissions, disclosures, facts.
2. **Native effectiveness review** — naturalness, emotional effect, clarity, cultural fit, CTA.

Premium public work with medium or high cultural distance should not pass solely through bilingual model review.

## Failure patterns

- literal hook translation that loses curiosity or rhythm;
- one “neutral” language variant used across incompatible markets;
- local slang added without audience fit;
- visual symbols copied from the source market;
- translated proof that is unavailable locally;
- source pacing retained despite longer target-language speech;
- localization performed after picture lock with no timing flexibility.

## Cost controls

Localize the concept and script before final production. Use modular masters. Test one or two high-value markets before producing every locale. Reuse approved terminology and voice profiles, but refresh trend-dependent phrasing.
