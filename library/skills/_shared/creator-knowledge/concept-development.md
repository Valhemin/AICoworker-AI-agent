# Concept Development Playbook

## Concept definition

A concept is not a topic. It is a specific creative mechanism that delivers a promise to an audience.

```yaml
concept:
  premise:
  audience_tension:
  promise:
  mechanism:
  emotional_engine:
  visual_or_audio_device:
  proof_or_payoff:
  format_fit:
  repeatability:
  production_risk:
  differentiation:
```

## Concept mechanisms

Use distinct mechanisms so variants are genuinely different:

- reversal: expected outcome is challenged;
- experiment: question is resolved through a test;
- transformation: before/after with causal proof;
- confession: hidden tension is revealed;
- challenge: a constraint drives action;
- countdown: limited time or ranked sequence;
- mystery: clues lead to a reveal;
- comparison: alternatives are tested under the same conditions;
- simulation: “what would happen if…”;
- character dilemma: choice exposes value;
- visual metaphor: an abstract idea becomes visible;
- documentary access: audience sees a process normally hidden;
- participation: viewers predict, choose, or respond.

## Concept slate rule

Generate concepts across mechanisms, not five rewrites of one idea. For each slate include:

- one low-cost proven format;
- one differentiated adjacent format;
- one high-upside experiment;
- additional premium options when quality mode requires them.

## Screening tests

1. **One-sentence clarity** — can a producer explain the experience without jargon?
2. **Promise test** — what exact value is received?
3. **Mechanism test** — what happens on screen or in audio?
4. **Proof test** — why should the audience believe or feel it?
5. **Distinctiveness test** — is the difference structural or cosmetic?
6. **Producibility test** — can tools, budget, time, and rights support it?
7. **Platform test** — does it work in the native viewing context?
8. **Series test** — if repeatability matters, what changes each episode?

## Scoring matrix

Score 1–5:

- audience relevance;
- clarity;
- emotional or intellectual pull;
- differentiation;
- visual/audio potential;
- proof strength;
- platform fit;
- production feasibility;
- cost/value ratio;
- localization resilience.

Do not average away a fatal flaw. Rights, truth, or infeasibility can veto a high-scoring idea.

## Kill and repair rules

Kill or substantially change a concept when:

- hook cannot be paid off;
- proof is unavailable;
- the concept depends on deception or unsafe imitation;
- differentiation disappears when trend styling is removed;
- production requires identity or motion consistency beyond available tools;
- target-market meaning fails in local review.

Repair by changing the mechanism, reducing scope, replacing proof, shifting format, or choosing a different target segment—not by adding adjectives to the prompt.

## Acceptance criteria

The artifact is decision-ready, exposes assumptions and unresolved risk, follows the named target market and routine, and gives downstream owners enough structured information to proceed without inventing critical details.
