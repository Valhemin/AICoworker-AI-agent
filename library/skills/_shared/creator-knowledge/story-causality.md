# Story Causality Playbook

## Story engine

A story is a chain of pressured choices and consequences, not a sequence of attractive events.

Core variables:

- **want**: what the character consciously pursues;
- **need**: the change required for deeper resolution;
- **misbelief or limitation**: the pattern blocking change;
- **stakes**: specific loss, cost, or irreversible consequence;
- **opposition**: person, system, environment, self, or time;
- **choice**: action that reveals value;
- **consequence**: new state caused by the choice.

## Beat test

For each beat ask:

1. What changes?
2. What caused it?
3. What new pressure or option appears?
4. What decision follows?
5. Why can the story not return to the previous state?

If a beat can be removed without changing later choices, it is exposition, repetition, or decoration.

A scene should usually end with a changed plan, relationship, knowledge state, power balance, or risk.

## Escalation levers

Escalation is not only “bigger.” Increase pressure by:

- reducing time;
- narrowing options;
- increasing personal cost;
- exposing hidden information;
- forcing public commitment;
- damaging a relationship;
- making success create a new problem;
- turning an external obstacle into an internal choice;
- removing the character’s reliable tactic.

## Setup and payoff

Track setup type, visibility, expected distance, and payoff mode:

- object or prop;
- behavior or habit;
- visual motif;
- line of dialogue;
- rule of the world;
- relationship promise;
- false assumption;
- skill or limitation.

A payoff should feel surprising in the moment but inevitable in retrospect. Avoid explaining it after the audience already understands.

## Compression by duration

- **15–30 seconds**: one tension, one turn, one payoff.
- **30–90 seconds**: 3–7 beats, one major escalation, clear proof or emotional reversal.
- **2–5 minutes**: a complete short arc, limited subplots, strong visual causality.
- **8–20 minutes**: sequences with internal turns, retention resets, secondary complications.
- **episodic**: each episode pays a local promise while advancing a season engine.

Do not compress by removing causality. Remove backstory, secondary characters, repeated proof, and decorative transitions first.

## Character arc patterns

- positive change: limitation is confronted and behavior changes;
- negative change: pressure deepens the limitation;
- flat arc: stable truth changes the world or another character;
- disillusionment: false belief is lost without easy replacement;
- relationship arc: trust, power, dependence, or intimacy changes.

## Failure patterns

- coincidence solves the central problem;
- antagonist or obstacle disappears;
- climax is spectacle without a decision;
- twist introduces new information with no setup;
- character states the theme instead of embodying it;
- stakes are abstract and unchanged by failure;
- montage substitutes for conflict.

## Quality gate

A story passes when the audience can trace why each major event happened, the climax forces a meaningful choice, and the ending pays the original emotional or informational promise.
