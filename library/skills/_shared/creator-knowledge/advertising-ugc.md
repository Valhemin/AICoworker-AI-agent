# Advertising and UGC Playbook

## Commercial truth

An ad must connect audience tension, offer, proof, objection, and action. It cannot use cinematic polish to hide a weak or false claim.

## Awareness levels

- unaware: surface a recognizable situation without demanding product knowledge;
- problem-aware: clarify cost and mechanism;
- solution-aware: differentiate approach;
- product-aware: address proof, objections, and offer;
- most aware: reduce friction and add timely reason to act.

## UGC structure options

1. **Problem → discovery → demonstration → result → CTA**
2. **Objection → test → evidence → verdict**
3. **Mistake/confession → new method → proof**
4. **Comparison → criterion → winner → who it is for**
5. **Day-in-the-life integration → friction removed**

UGC should feel native but remain truthful and disclosed when required. “Natural” does not mean hiding sponsorship.

## Proof library

- live demonstration;
- quantified result with conditions;
- process transparency;
- third-party source;
- customer story with consent;
- side-by-side comparison under fair conditions;
- guarantee or risk reversal;
- expert explanation;
- product details tied to outcome.

Proof must match the strength of the claim.

Do not wait until the final CTA to address the primary objection.

## Performance creative system

Build variations across:

- angle;
- hook mechanism;
- creator archetype;
- proof order;
- format;
- offer framing;
- CTA;
- duration.

A useful test matrix changes one major variable or intentionally tests a package, and records downstream conversion quality.

## Brand versus direct response

Brand work builds memory structures, meaning, and distinctiveness. Direct response reduces uncertainty and friction. A single asset can do both, but it must not sacrifice accurate proof for emotion or make the product invisible until the end.

## Failure patterns

- generic pain point unrelated to product mechanism;
- testimonial written in corporate language;
- dramatic result with no conditions or evidence;
- fake comments or fabricated social proof;
- product inserted after the story rather than causing change;
- too many benefits for the duration;
- CTA asks for commitment beyond the audience’s awareness level.

## Cost controls

Test script and rough performance before expensive visual production. Build modular hooks, proof inserts, CTAs, and product shots. Create paid-media variants only when the campaign can generate enough impressions or conversions to learn.
