# Dialogue and Humanization Playbook

## Human dialogue is goal-directed

People speak to get something, protect something, test something, avoid something, or change how they are seen. The literal sentence is only one layer.

For each speaker define:

```yaml
voice_profile:
  social_role:
  relationship_to_listener:
  immediate_intent:
  status_strategy:
  education_or_expertise:
  vocabulary_range:
  sentence_length:
  directness:
  humor_style:
  taboo_or_avoidance:
  verbal_tics_to_use_sparingly: []
  what_they_never_say_directly:
```

## Subtext methods

- answer a different question;
- minimize or exaggerate;
- use practical talk to avoid emotion;
- change subject at pressure points;
- repeat a word with changed meaning;
- let silence or action contradict speech;
- make politeness carry threat or distance;
- let a character reveal more than intended.

Subtext must still be comprehensible through context and performance.

## Naturalness without mess

Real speech contains fragments, repairs, overlaps, fillers, and repetition, but indiscriminate transcription is tiring. Use disfluency strategically for hesitation, status, emotion, or character identity.

## Distinct voices

Test by removing names from ten lines. If the speaker is impossible to infer, increase distinction through intent, worldview, vocabulary, rhythm, and what each character notices—not accent caricature.

## Global and localized dialogue

- specify spoken versus written register;
- distinguish dialect choice from stereotype;
- validate honorifics and relationship language;
- preserve timing and breath after transcreation;
- avoid importing English punchline structure into languages with different syntax;
- use native review for comedy, slang, intimate dialogue, and culturally sensitive scenes.

## AI-writing symptoms to remove

- balanced three-part sentences everywhere;
- generic wisdom and motivational summary;
- explicit emotional labels;
- repeated “the truth is,” “what if I told you,” or equivalent clichés;
- every response perfectly addresses the previous line;
- excessive rhetorical questions;
- no interruption, misunderstanding, or private agenda;
- characters who exist only to deliver information.

## Read-aloud protocol

1. Generate a scratch performance at realistic speed.
2. Mark lines that are hard to breathe, act, or understand.
3. Identify duplicate information.
4. Check whether emotion rises because of events, not adjectives.
5. Verify target-language timing and subtitle load.
6. Preserve intentional silence and reaction time.

## Brand voice

A brand voice profile needs observable rules:

- level of certainty;
- degree of formality;
- sentence rhythm;
- humor boundaries;
- evidence behavior;
- vocabulary preferences;
- relationship to audience;
- forbidden claims and clichés.

“Friendly and professional” is too vague to guide writing.

## Acceptance criteria

The artifact is decision-ready, exposes assumptions and unresolved risk, follows the named target market and routine, and gives downstream owners enough structured information to proceed without inventing critical details.
