# Podcast and Interview Production Playbook

## Purpose

Create interviews and podcasts that reveal useful thinking, experience, conflict, or story rather than producing a chronological résumé or a list of generic questions.

## Editorial premise

Define the listener job, episode promise, guest’s unique access, central tension, evidence needs, market/language, duration, tone, and what would make the episode meaningfully different from existing interviews.

## Research dossier

Prepare:

- verified biography and pronunciation;
- prior public positions and recurring stories;
- expertise boundaries and conflicts of interest;
- projects, dates, claims, and source links;
- contradictions, unanswered questions, and sensitive areas;
- topics already over-covered;
- release, consent, embargo, and sponsor constraints.

## Question architecture

Use a sequence, not a questionnaire:

1. orientation: establish stakes and relevance quickly;
2. concrete scene or decision: ask for what happened, not only opinions;
3. mechanism: how and why;
4. pressure: tradeoffs, failure, disagreement, or uncertainty;
5. reflection: what changed;
6. application: what the listener can carry forward.

Prepare follow-up branches:

- “What happened next?”
- “What did you see or hear?”
- “What made that difficult?”
- “What evidence changed your mind?”
- “What would someone who disagrees say?”
- “Can you give a specific example?”

Do not read every prepared question when the answer creates a stronger path.

## Host behavior

Listen for nouns, decisions, claims, contradictions, emotion, and missing causality. Interrupt only to clarify, protect time, correct harm, or deepen the answer. Avoid performing knowledge at the guest’s expense. State corrections transparently.

## Remote and studio capture

- record isolated local tracks when possible;
- monitor clipping, noise, room echo, drift, and connection failure;
- capture room tone and backup program feed;
- test remote platform, browser permissions, headphones, and file upload;
- log timecodes for standout moments and corrections;
- obtain consent for recording, editing, promotion, synthetic processing, and clips as applicable.

## Edit principles

Preserve meaning and chronology where they matter. Remove repetition, false starts, technical delay, and irrelevant detours, but do not manufacture agreement or certainty. Mark fact-check inserts and post-record corrections. Use music and sound sparingly around speech.

## Packaging and repurposing

Create title/cover promise, description, chapters, transcript, source/correction notes, quote cards, clips, article/newsletter derivative, and CTA. Each clip needs enough context to remain accurate.

## Localization

Choose subtitles, dubbed summary, translated transcript, or market-specific edit based on audience behavior and cost. Re-check names, technical terms, jokes, sensitive topics, and legal claims. Voice cloning requires explicit permission and disclosure where appropriate.

## Failure recovery

- generic answers: ask for a scene, decision, example, cost, or evidence;
- overlong episode: cut by listener value and sequence function, not evenly;
- weak host: use concise bridges and let the guest carry the evidence;
- factual dispute: pause publication or add a verified correction;
- unusable audio: prioritize intelligibility, transparent repair, transcript, or rerecord critical lines.

## Acceptance criteria

The episode fulfills a specific listener promise, reveals concrete information or story, preserves guest meaning, has clean and accessible audio, resolves fact/rights issues, and produces context-safe derivatives.
