# Live Content Production Playbook

## Purpose

Design livestreams, webinars, live commerce, panels, classes, launches, and interactive broadcasts that remain coherent under uncertainty and produce reusable post-live assets.

## Live brief

Specify objective, audience, markets/languages, platform, duration, host/guest roles, moderation policy, CTA, offers, legal disclosures, interaction features, technical stack, fallback channels, and post-live deliverables.

## Rundown design

```yaml
segment:
  start_time:
  duration:
  objective:
  lead:
  content_or_demo:
  audience_interaction:
  assets_cued:
  CTA_or_transition:
  hard_stop:
  fallback:
```

Use hard and soft timing. Hard segments include paid commitments, guest windows, legal disclosures, offers, and platform cutoffs. Soft segments can expand or contract.

## Attention rhythm

Reset attention through meaningful change: new question, demonstration, guest, camera mode, audience decision, visual aid, result, challenge, or summary. Do not manufacture constant noise. Re-state context for late arrivals without restarting the entire program.

## Host preparation

Give hosts:

- opening promise and first interaction;
- segment objectives rather than full memorized paragraphs;
- transitions, pronunciations, facts, disclaimers, and names;
- approved improvisation boundaries;
- recovery lines for silence, delay, error, or off-topic comments;
- clear hand signals and producer communication protocol.

## Interaction design

Use chat, polls, Q&A, reactions, submissions, live choices, or challenges only when the response changes the program or creates evidence of learning/interest. Define moderation and privacy handling before collecting user content.

## Technical plan

- primary and backup internet/audio/capture;
- record isolated audio and clean program feed when possible;
- local recording if platform recording is unreliable;
- scene/source naming and cue sheets;
- latency expectations;
- accessible captions and language interpretation;
- fallback slate, holding content, or alternate URL;
- test stream in the actual platform environment.

## Live commerce and claims

Lock prices, stock caveats, offer windows, product claims, sponsorship disclosure, refund/availability language, and market restrictions. A host must know which statements require exact approved wording.

## Incident protocol

Classify incidents:

- technical degradation;
- factual mistake;
- guest/talent issue;
- harmful or illegal audience content;
- privacy leak;
- rights violation;
- safety emergency.

For each, define who can mute, cut, remove, correct, end, or publish a statement. Record the incident and downstream edit implications.

## Post-live pipeline

Create transcript, chapters, highlights, corrections, source links, clipped derivatives, quote approvals, captions, thumbnail/title tests, and an experiment report. Do not clip a statement out of context or leave a corrected false claim in derivatives.

## Acceptance criteria

The rundown has objectives, timing, ownership, interactions, fallbacks, disclosures, accessibility, incident authority, recording redundancy, and a post-live reuse plan.
