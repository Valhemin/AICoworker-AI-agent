# Documentary Research and Narrative Playbook

## Evidence categories

Label every meaningful element:

- verified fact;
- attributed testimony;
- interpretation;
- inference;
- disputed claim;
- reconstruction;
- illustrative visualization;
- fiction or dramatization.

The visual style must not cause a reconstruction to be mistaken for archival evidence.

## Claim ledger

```yaml
claim:
  claim_id:
  exact_wording:
  importance: critical|supporting|context
  source_ids: []
  source_tier:
  corroboration:
  counter_evidence: []
  uncertainty:
  allowed_visualization:
  disclosure_needed:
  last_verified:
```

Critical claims should favor primary sources and independent corroboration. Source quantity does not compensate for low quality or common copying.

## Research workflow

1. Define the central question and boundaries.
2. Build a timeline and entity map.
3. Identify claims that would change the story if false.
4. Gather primary sources and direct testimony.
5. Seek disconfirming evidence and affected viewpoints.
6. Record provenance and rights for documents, images, footage, and audio.
7. Separate what is known from what is narratively attractive.
8. Build a treatment only after the evidence map is stable enough.

## Narrative structure

Documentary structure can use mystery, chronology, character, system, investigation, comparison, or thesis. The structure may control reveal order but cannot distort evidence.

Useful sequence design:

- question;
- current stakes;
- evidence or access;
- complication or contradiction;
- deeper mechanism;
- affected people;
- limits and uncertainty;
- responsible conclusion.

## Interviews

Prepare questions that elicit experience, mechanism, chronology, and evidence. Avoid feeding the desired quote. Distinguish factual witness, expert interpretation, stakeholder advocacy, and personal opinion.

## Generated media

Allowed uses may include maps, diagrams, abstract transitions, clearly labeled reconstructions, and illustrative scenes. Avoid generating fake archival footage, fabricated documents, or false likenesses presented as real.

## Fairness and harm

- give subjects a meaningful opportunity to respond when allegations are material;
- minimize unnecessary exposure of private individuals;
- assess whether visual reconstruction increases harm or certainty;
- avoid compressing uncertainty into a definitive villain narrative;
- preserve context for quotations.

## Failure patterns

- script written before claim verification;
- secondary source copied as primary fact;
- dramatic chronology that implies false causation;
- AI image used as evidence;
- lack of counter-evidence search;
- “both sides” symmetry when evidence quality is unequal;
- legal review omitted for high-risk allegations.
