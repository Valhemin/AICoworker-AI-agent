# Suspense and Horror Playbook

## Purpose

Create controlled uncertainty, dread, fear, and release through information, anticipation, vulnerability, consequence, and sensory design—not only darkness, jump scares, or graphic imagery.

## Distinguish the effects

- **suspense**: the audience anticipates a consequential outcome;
- **mystery**: the audience seeks an explanation for what happened;
- **surprise**: information arrives without extended anticipation;
- **dread**: the audience expects something bad but cannot fully control when or how;
- **horror**: a violation threatens body, identity, reality, morality, or belonging.

A scene may combine these, but the information plan must be explicit.

## Information map

```yaml
threat_beat:
  audience_knows:
  character_knows:
  believed_rule:
  actual_rule:
  warning_signal:
  available_escape:
  cost_of_action:
  reveal_or_reversal:
```

Suspense often strengthens when the audience knows more than the character; mystery often strengthens when both lack a key explanation.

## Threat design

Define:

- capability;
- limitation;
- detection rule;
- motive or pattern, even if not fully explained;
- escalation behavior;
- evidence left behind;
- why ordinary escape fails;
- what human weakness the threat pressures.

Changing the rules for convenience destroys fear. Unknown does not mean arbitrary.

## Escalation ladder

1. anomaly that can be dismissed;
2. pattern that implies intention;
3. personal cost or isolation;
4. failed safe tactic;
5. revelation that changes the rule;
6. choice under irreversible pressure;
7. aftermath that resolves or deepens the core fear.

Use fewer stages for short form, but preserve causal progression.

## Visual and sound language

- frame negative space with a reason;
- control what is visible, audible, and inferred;
- use offscreen sound to create geography;
- repeat a motif, then alter one feature;
- let silence reset the audience before impact;
- avoid constant music that tells the audience how to feel;
- design light sources and exits into blocking so fear emerges from space.

## Character vulnerability

Fear becomes specific when the threat attacks a valued relationship, role, memory, identity, body function, or moral boundary. Give the character a tactic and let the threat learn or counter it.

## Market and safety review

Validate age rating, violence, self-harm implications, religious/folk imagery, trauma triggers, platform rules, and cultural meaning in each target market. Do not present harmful instructions or deceptive real-event reconstructions as entertainment.

## AI production strategy

Generated horror often fails through unintended anatomy or continuity. Decide whether distortion is intentional. Use shorter shots, controlled silhouettes, inserts, practical textures, sound-led suggestion, and approved keyframes. Track threat invariants in the world bible.

## Failure recovery

- only jump scares: add anticipation, information asymmetry, and consequence;
- arbitrary monster: define rules and evidence;
- passive protagonist: create choices and failed tactics;
- confusing geography: establish exits, distances, and sound position;
- overexposure: reduce visual access or change the threat rather than repeating it;
- weak ending: force a choice that resolves the human fear, not merely the external attack.

## Acceptance criteria

The threat follows a coherent rule, audience information is deliberately controlled, escalation changes options, sound/space support the effect, and the ending pays the central fear or question.
