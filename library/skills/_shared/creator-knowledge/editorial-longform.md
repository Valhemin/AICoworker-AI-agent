# Editorial Long-form Playbook

## Purpose

Create articles, newsletters, guides, essays, and thought leadership that reward sustained attention with evidence, reasoning, specificity, and a useful conclusion.

## Brief variables

- reader segment and current knowledge;
- reader job: decide, understand, compare, learn, act, or feel seen;
- thesis or central question;
- publication, market, locale, and editorial voice;
- evidence standard and freshness window;
- target length, deadline, CTA, and distribution package.

## Research architecture

Build a claim ledger before drafting:

```yaml
claim:
  id:
  wording:
  importance: critical|supporting|context
  evidence_ids: []
  source_quality:
  freshness:
  counter_evidence:
  uncertainty:
  allowed_strength:
```

Distinguish reported fact, expert interpretation, author analysis, anecdote, and prediction. Do not hide inference behind authoritative prose.

## Structure options

- problem → causes → options → recommendation;
- question → evidence journey → answer → implications;
- thesis → objections → synthesis;
- case narrative → pattern → framework → application;
- comparison → criteria → tradeoffs → decision guide;
- chronological investigation with explicit evidence status.

Choose the structure that serves the reader’s decision, not the writer’s research order.

## Section contract

Each section should have:

- a question or function;
- one primary claim;
- evidence or example;
- interpretation;
- transition showing why the next section matters.

A section that only repeats the thesis in new words should be cut or merged.

## Voice and readability

- vary sentence length according to reasoning and emphasis;
- use concrete nouns and active verbs where accurate;
- define technical terms at first meaningful use;
- avoid inflated certainty, generic scene-setting, and “in today’s fast-paced world” openings;
- use headings that reveal progression rather than label broad topics;
- preserve nuance without forcing every paragraph to contain a caveat.

## Openings

Open with a consequential question, precise observation, surprising verified fact, scene, conflict, or decision. The first paragraphs must establish relevance and the article’s actual promise. Avoid suspense that withholds the topic.

## Evidence and examples

Use examples to test or clarify a claim, not decorate it. One detailed example may teach more than a list of shallow examples. Include counterexamples when they change the decision boundary.

## Global and local publication

For multi-market editions, decide among translation, localization, transcreation, and original-local rewrite. Revalidate claims, links, product availability, legal terminology, dates, units, examples, and cultural assumptions. Keep a parent/child artifact relationship so corrections propagate.

## Packaging

Deliver title families, deck/subtitle, excerpt, social copy, email subject/preheader, featured-image brief, metadata, internal links, source list, and update date. Titles must match the conclusion rather than oversell the most dramatic detail.

## Quality gate

- thesis or question is explicit;
- every critical claim maps to adequate evidence;
- counter-evidence or uncertainty is represented when material;
- sections progress rather than repeat;
- the conclusion changes understanding or action;
- links, citations, rights, and locale details are checked;
- the piece has a defined update/expiry policy for unstable information.
