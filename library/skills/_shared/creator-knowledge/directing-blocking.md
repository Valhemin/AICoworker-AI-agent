# Directing and Blocking Playbook

## Directing question

What should the audience understand or feel because of how people and objects behave in space—not because the script explains it?

## Performance direction

Give playable actions rather than emotional adjectives.

Weak: “be sad.”
Stronger: “keep the task precise so the other person cannot see your hands shaking.”

Direction can specify objective, obstacle, tactic, physical action, attention target, rhythm, and change point.

## Blocking principles

- movement should arise from intention or pressure;
- distance communicates intimacy, threat, status, or avoidance;
- entrances and exits alter power;
- eyelines and body orientation control alliances;
- foreground/background placement can reveal exclusion or surveillance;
- props can externalize negotiation and control;
- stillness is strongest when movement has established a pattern.

## Generated-character constraints

Design actions that current tools can hold:

- prefer one dominant action per shot;
- avoid complex hand-object interactions unless essential and benchmarked;
- split difficult choreography into coverage;
- use reaction shots, inserts, silhouettes, or off-screen action when reliability is low;
- define start and end poses for image-to-video;
- maintain screen direction and spatial logic across generated clips.

## Coverage strategy

Capture the story turn, not just the dialogue:

- master or geographic anchor when needed;
- objective/action coverage;
- reaction and decision coverage;
- inserts for causal details;
- transition shot only if it supports time, space, or tone.

## Failure patterns

- actors walk randomly to create motion;
- every line receives a new angle;
- blocking ignores lens and frame size;
- generated shot asks for simultaneous complex actions;
- emotional climax has no reaction time;
- character position changes between coverage with no motivated movement.
