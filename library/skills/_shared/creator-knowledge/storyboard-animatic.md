# Storyboard and Animatic Playbook

## Storyboard purpose

A storyboard is a decision tool for coverage, geography, timing, continuity, and production risk. It is not a gallery of final-looking images.

## Panel requirements

Each panel should show:

- shot ID and scene ID;
- frame and aspect ratio;
- subject positions and eyelines;
- dominant action;
- camera movement arrow if any;
- dialogue/VO or key sound;
- estimated duration;
- transition or cut reason;
- continuity and generation notes.

## Coverage audit

Before generating final assets, verify:

- opening orientation is clear;
- key causal details are visible;
- character decisions have reaction coverage;
- geography supports action;
- inserts are available for difficult transitions;
- the edit can shorten or extend without breaking logic;
- platform crops preserve essential information;
- high-risk shots have fallbacks.

## Animatic timing

Use scratch audio and real reading speed. Add pauses, reaction, visual comprehension, and transition time. The animatic should reveal whether the script is too long before expensive rendering.

## Batch versus sequential workflow

- batch boards: concept exploration, composition alternatives, mood and coverage discovery;
- sequential boards: locked character continuity, scene-state control, final keyframes.

Do not claim that one batch image guarantees final consistency.

## Board styles

Choose based on decision:

- rough thumbnails for speed;
- line boards for action clarity;
- grayscale tone boards for lighting and value;
- photo boards for location/brand realism;
- generated keyframes for model feasibility;
- motion tests for difficult camera or body action.

## Failure patterns

- beautiful boards that omit screen direction;
- identical composition across every panel;
- no shot duration or audio;
- storyboard inherits script problems rather than exposing them;
- generated keyframes silently alter character canon;
- edit is locked before testing voice timing.
