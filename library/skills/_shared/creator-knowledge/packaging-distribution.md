# Packaging and Distribution Playbook

## Package alignment

Title, thumbnail/cover, opening, content, and CTA must describe the same promise. Packaging can increase curiosity but must not recruit the wrong audience.

## Title functions

- orient topic and audience;
- state or imply benefit;
- create a meaningful information gap;
- signal specificity or evidence;
- distinguish from category clichés.

Avoid vague superlatives, false exclusivity, and titles that the content cannot satisfy.

## Thumbnail/key-art system

A thumbnail needs one dominant idea at small size:

- focal subject or object;
- readable emotional or informational contrast;
- limited text if any;
- clean silhouette and value separation;
- distinctiveness from neighboring content;
- accurate representation of the video.

Create variants that test different mechanisms, not minor color changes.

## Distribution design

Decide organic, paid, partner/creator, search, email, community, and owned-site roles. The same asset may require different context and landing paths.

## A/B testing

- pre-register the variable and primary metric;
- create sufficiently different variants;
- avoid declaring a winner with insufficient exposure;
- judge downstream watch time or conversion quality, not only clicks;
- record audience and time-window differences;
- retain “no meaningful difference” as a valid result.

## Failure patterns

- package promises a result not demonstrated;
- cover text duplicates title without adding information;
- localization changes words but not visual meaning;
- CTA is inconsistent with funnel stage;
- Shorts package is evaluated like long-form thumbnail behavior;
- paid and organic variants share claims but not disclosure rules.
