# Hook and Retention Playbook

## Retention is a promise-management system

The opening must answer three questions quickly enough for the viewing context:

1. What is this about?
2. Why should this audience care now?
3. What value, proof, reveal, or feeling will arrive if they continue?

Mystery without orientation creates confusion. Clarity without tension creates indifference.

## Hook mechanisms

- consequential question;
- contradiction or surprising result;
- visual anomaly;
- immediate demonstration;
- specific problem recognition;
- high-stakes choice;
- credible confession;
- time constraint;
- comparison with a meaningful difference;
- unfinished transformation;
- challenge or prediction;
- access to a hidden process.

Avoid unsupported superlatives and false urgency.

## Retention map

Mark where the audience receives new value:

- orientation;
- first proof;
- escalation;
- reframe or reveal;
- emotional reaction;
- practical application;
- payoff;
- next-step bridge.

A reset is justified when attention has a new reason to continue, not simply because the editor adds a zoom or sound effect.

## Short-form pacing

Pacing depends on information, emotion, and motion. Do not apply a fixed cut every two seconds. Hold longer for reaction, suspense, beauty, or comprehension. Cut faster when each image is simple and cumulative.

## Long-form retention

Use chapter-level questions and progressive value:

- cold open previews the experience without giving away all proof;
- early section establishes credibility and map;
- each sequence resolves one question and opens the next;
- pattern changes are motivated by content;
- recap only when cognitive load requires it;
- the ending delivers the title/thumbnail promise and creates a relevant next-view path.

## Diagnostic patterns

- early drop: weak orientation, mismatch, slow setup, poor sound, or wrong audience;
- mid-video drop: repeated point, delayed proof, low consequence, or confusing structure;
- spike: replay, surprise, useful detail, or accidental ambiguity;
- strong CTR but weak watch time: packaging overpromises or attracts the wrong viewer;
- strong watch time but low action: value is entertaining but CTA, offer, or relevance is weak.

## Experiment design

Test mechanisms, not punctuation. Examples:

- demo-first vs confession-first;
- outcome-first vs process-first;
- expert proof vs peer proof;
- tension framed as loss vs aspiration;
- host-led vs object-led opening.

Keep the content body stable when testing the package unless the test explicitly includes both.

## Acceptance criteria

The artifact is decision-ready, exposes assumptions and unresolved risk, follows the named target market and routine, and gives downstream owners enough structured information to proceed without inventing critical details.
