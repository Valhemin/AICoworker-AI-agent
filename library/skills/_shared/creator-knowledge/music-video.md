# Music Video Creative and Production Playbook

## Purpose

Design a music video whose visual progression is motivated by the song, artist identity, audience, rights, and production reality. A collection of attractive shots is not a treatment.

## Required inputs

- final or near-final song master, BPM, duration, and section timecodes;
- music/master rights and performer/likeness permissions;
- artist brand, era, audience, release objective, target markets, and platform cuts;
- performance, narrative, conceptual, choreography, VFX, and location constraints;
- budget, schedule, available capture/generation tools, and required deliverables.

## Song map

Create a timecoded map before visual ideation:

```yaml
song_section:
  start:
  end:
  label: intro|verse|pre|chorus|bridge|drop|outro
  lyrical_function:
  energy_1_to_5:
  instrumentation_change:
  vocal_event:
  visual_change_required:
  edit_density:
```

Visual change should respond to meaningful musical change, not every beat. Reserve contrast for structurally important moments.

## Treatment modes

- **performance-led**: presence, charisma, choreography, lighting, and coverage carry the piece;
- **narrative-led**: a causal story progresses alongside or against the lyrics;
- **concept-led**: one visual rule evolves through variation and escalation;
- **hybrid**: performance anchors comprehension while narrative or concept creates progression;
- **lyric/graphic**: typography, motion design, illustration, or archive is the primary language.

Choose one dominant mode. Hybrids fail when each mode competes for equal attention.

## Visual progression design

Track at least four dimensions across the song:

1. scale: intimate to expansive or the reverse;
2. movement: static, handheld, dolly, crane, choreography, or generated motion;
3. color/light: controlled shifts tied to emotional sections;
4. space/state: locations, costume states, props, weather, or visual transformations.

The final chorus should not merely repeat the first chorus unless repetition is the concept. Change camera access, performance intensity, crowd, environment, or edit relationship.

## Performance direction

- define the performer’s objective for each section, not only “more energy”;
- capture full-song safety takes plus section-specific coverage;
- protect lip-sync with playback speed, frame-rate, and audio reference logs;
- plan eye line and lens distance to control intimacy;
- design gestures and choreography for framing and editability;
- record continuity notes for hair, sweat, costume, prop, and performance intensity.

## Narrative integration

Lyrics may be literal, associative, ironic, or independent. Decide deliberately. Avoid illustrating every noun. Narrative beats must remain understandable without forcing the performer to explain them.

## AI-assisted production

Use AI where it creates distinctive value or solves a production constraint:

- concept frames and treatment boards;
- impossible environments or controlled transformations;
- stylized inserts, transitions, or visual motifs;
- background extensions and selected VFX plates;
- image-to-video for shots whose start frame and motion can be tightly specified.

Do not generate long sequences before approving a song-map animatic. Benchmark identity, lip detail, choreography, hands, fast motion, and frame-to-frame design consistency on the hardest representative shots.

## Editing rules

- cut on phrase, breath, gesture, lyric turn, instrumentation, or visual cause—not by a fixed beat grid alone;
- alternate anticipation and release;
- allow hero performance moments to breathe;
- use repeated framings as motifs only when their change is meaningful;
- create clean, explicit, vertical, square, teaser, and looping deliverables from a master timeline;
- QC sync after every speed change, frame interpolation, or platform transcode.

## Rights and safety gate

Confirm master/composition permissions, performer releases, choreography/location/artwork rights, archive licenses, synthetic likeness/voice permissions, disclosure obligations, and market-specific restrictions before final publication.

## Failure recovery

- weak progression: rebuild the song map and reserve visual escalation for structural peaks;
- random imagery: reduce to one dominant visual mechanism and two supporting motifs;
- poor lip-sync: prioritize captured performance or shorter controlled generated segments;
- unaffordable concept: preserve the mechanism using fewer locations, reusable sets, projection, motion graphics, or strategic inserts;
- identity drift: use reference-conditioned workflows, shorter shots, cutaways, and verified continuity states.

## Acceptance criteria

The treatment states why each major visual change occurs, the artist remains the center of meaning, the final deliverables are technically feasible, rights are traceable, and the song has a clear visual arc from opening image to final state.
