# Content Repurposing Playbook

## Repurposing is re-authoring

A derivative must fit the new audience state, platform, duration, and content job. Cropping and shortening are only mechanical transformations.

## Source decomposition

Break the source into atomic units:

- claims and evidence;
- stories and examples;
- demonstrations;
- quotes;
- objections and answers;
- visuals and B-roll;
- frameworks and steps;
- hooks and open questions;
- CTAs.

Assign source IDs so derivatives can trace claims and updates.

## Adaptation rules

- preserve evidence context when shortening;
- write a new hook for the derivative’s promise;
- do not create certainty by removing caveats;
- change pacing and visual grammar for platform;
- use the strongest native proof available;
- avoid publishing many near-duplicates that fatigue the audience;
- update all children when a source claim changes.

## Content cascade example

One researched long-form episode may produce:

- 3 short clips with distinct promises;
- one visual framework carousel;
- one newsletter with deeper context;
- a FAQ from objections;
- a sales or learning asset only if the source supports that job.

The number of outputs is not a success metric.

## Failure patterns

- derivative starts mid-context;
- title overstates a nuanced source;
- clip contains a question but not its answer;
- same caption and CTA used everywhere;
- local-market version reuses irrelevant examples;
- rights to source footage do not cover derivative ads.

## Acceptance criteria

The artifact is decision-ready, exposes assumptions and unresolved risk, follows the named target market and routine, and gives downstream owners enough structured information to proceed without inventing critical details.
