# Creator Brand System Playbook

## Purpose

Build a recognizable, repeatable creator identity that guides topic selection, voice, formats, visuals, community behavior, partnerships, and adaptation without turning every output into the same template.

## Brand foundation

Define:

- audience relationship: teacher, peer, investigator, entertainer, curator, coach, challenger, host, or hybrid;
- value promise: what reliably changes for the audience;
- earned authority: experience, access, method, taste, results, or community;
- point of view: beliefs and tradeoffs, not generic values;
- emotional experience: how the audience should feel during and after;
- boundaries: topics, tactics, sponsors, claims, and behaviors that are off-brand.

Include annotated examples and counterexamples. Adjectives such as “authentic” and “professional” are not sufficient instructions.

## Content architecture

Use three levels:

1. **pillars**: durable audience problems or interests;
2. **series/formats**: repeatable mechanisms with recognizable packaging;
3. **episodes/assets**: individual executions driven by a current question or opportunity.

Limit pillars enough to create memory. Each series needs a job, cadence, production cost, success metric, and retirement rule.

## Signature devices

A signature may be a question pattern, visual composition, demonstration method, recurring character, sound cue, editing rhythm, evidence format, or community ritual. It should reinforce the value promise, not merely decorate it.

## Global strategy

Decide whether the brand is:

- one global voice with localized packaging;
- a global core with market-specific examples and hosts;
- separate local channels under shared brand principles;
- original-local content with limited cross-market reuse.

Document which elements are invariant and which are adaptable. Humor, authority signals, self-disclosure, pacing, CTA, and community norms may require transcreation.

## Partnership and monetization fit

Evaluate sponsors and products against audience trust, evidence quality, content role, market availability, and disclosure requirements. A partnership can be commercially attractive but strategically damaging if it changes the perceived relationship with the audience.

## Brand memory and governance

Maintain approved examples, visual tokens, pronunciation/name rules, claim policies, sponsor history, audience feedback, retired patterns, and exceptions. Version the brand bible; do not overwrite the reason a rule changed.

## Failure patterns

- too broad to be remembered;
- copied creator mannerisms instead of an owned mechanism;
- every post uses the same hook or edit;
- authority claims exceed evidence;
- brand voice becomes a list of adjectives;
- local versions translate words but not relationship or humor;
- growth experiments undermine trust.

## Acceptance criteria

A new writer or agent can select a fitting topic, write recognizable but non-repetitive copy, choose a compatible format and sponsor, and explain which brand elements may change by market.
